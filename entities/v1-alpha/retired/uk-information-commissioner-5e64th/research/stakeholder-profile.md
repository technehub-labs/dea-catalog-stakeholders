## Definition

The Information Commissioner's Office is the UK's independent supervisory authority for information rights: it enforces the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, upholds information rights in the public interest, and regulates other information-rights regimes (privacy and electronic communications, freedom of information across public authorities).

The record's normative description (verbatim): *The UK Information Commissioner's Office, the supervisory authority for the UK GDPR, with enforcement powers over the enterprise's UK-facing processing.*

## Mandate and goals

- Uphold information rights in the public interest (the ICO's statutory mission).
- Regulate data protection under the UK GDPR / DPA 2018: promote good practice, monitor and enforce compliance.
- Provide authoritative guidance so organizations meet their obligations without needing regulatory intervention.
- Act against misuse of personal data: investigations, enforcement notices, and monetary penalties.

## What they do

- Publishes guidance, codes of practice, and audits for data protection compliance.
- Handles complaints from data subjects and investigates controllers/processors.
- Issues enforcement notices and fines (up to the UK GDPR's statutory maximums).
- Reports to Parliament on the state of information rights.

## Engagement with the enterprise

The enterprise's public processing surfaces (repositories, community site) engage UK GDPR obligations where UK data subjects are involved; the enforced-regime record anchors the relationship and the enterprise's privacy capabilities keep it answerable to this authority.
