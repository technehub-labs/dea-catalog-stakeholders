## Definition

The US Department of Justice is the federal executive department responsible for enforcing federal law: criminal prosecution, civil enforcement, and defense of the United States' legal interests. In this catalog the record carries two enforcement capacities: general federal justice enforcement, and the trade-secret capacity under the Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA) — which created a federal civil cause of action for trade-secret misappropriation that the DOJ prosecutes in its criminal dimension (the federal criminal trade-secret statute) and private litigants exercise civilly.

The record's normative description (verbatim): *The United States Department of Justice, the federal enforcement authority for the Defend Trade Secrets Act (2016), prosecuting civil and criminal trade-secret misappropriation in federal court.*

## Mandate and goals

- Enforce federal law and defend the interests of the United States (the department's mission).
- In the DTSA capacity: provide a uniform federal remedy for trade-secret misappropriation, alongside state law.
- Deter and prosecute federal crimes, including economic espionage and criminal trade-secret theft (18 U.S.C. § 1832).

## What they do

- Investigates and prosecutes federal crimes (including economic espionage and trade-secret theft).
- Brings and defends civil enforcement actions in the public interest.
- In the DTSA dimension: the federal trade-secret regime is enforced through federal courts, with DOJ criminal authority as its punitive arm (and civil seizure/remedies available to private parties).
- Operates component agencies (FBI for investigations; US Attorneys' offices for district enforcement).

## Engagement with the enterprise

The enterprise's undisclosed know-how is protected federally under the DTSA regime this authority anchors; the enforced-regime record is the relationship's anchor. The justice-enforcement capacity is recorded as the general federal enforcement context (its regulation-specific edges land with future regime records).
