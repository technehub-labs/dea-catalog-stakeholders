## Definition

The Office for Civil Rights is the US Department of Health and Human Services' enforcement arm for the HIPAA Privacy, Security, and Breach Notification Rules: it protects the rights of persons under HIPAA, investigates complaints and breaches, conducts compliance reviews, and imposes civil monetary penalties for violations involving protected health information.

The record's normative description (verbatim): *The US Department of Health and Human Services, Office for Civil Rights — the regulator that enforces the HIPAA Privacy and Security Rules for protected health information.*

## Mandate and goals

- Protect individuals' rights over their protected health information (privacy, access, amendment).
- Ensure covered entities and business associates comply with the Privacy, Security, and Breach Notification Rules.
- Provide guidance and technical assistance so compliance is achievable without enforcement action.

## What they do

- Investigates complaints filed by individuals and reports of breaches involving protected health information.
- Conducts compliance reviews and enforces via resolution agreements, corrective-action plans, and civil monetary penalties.
- Publishes guidance on the HIPAA rules (de-identification, security safeguards, breach analysis).
- Operates the breach-reporting portal that covered entities must notify under the Breach Notification Rule.

## Engagement with the enterprise

Wherever the enterprise's activities touch protected health information, the HIPAA regime this authority enforces binds; the enforced-regime record anchors the relationship, and the enterprise's privacy capabilities (assessments, breach handling, records) are the operational counterpart.
