# European data-protection authorities (EDPB and national DPAs) — stakeholder profile

> Defining the stakeholder: what the party is, its goals, what it does, and how
> it engages the enterprise. Per `docs/EVIDENCE-STANDARD.md`; the record's
> description remains the normative one-liner — this profile expands it.

## Definition

The European data-protection authorities are the collective of national supervisory authorities (one per EU member state, plus the EEA-EFTA authorities participating via the EEA Agreement) and the European Data Protection Board that coordinates them. Together they supervise GDPR compliance: the national authorities investigate, issue corrective measures and fines; the EDPB issues binding guidelines and ensures consistent interpretation across the EEA.

The record's normative description (verbatim): *The European data-protection authorities — the European Data Protection Board and the national supervisory authorities it coordinates — which enforce the GDPR within their jurisdictions and hold enforcement powers over the enterprise's EU-facing processing.*

## Mandate and goals

- Protect natural persons' fundamental rights to personal-data protection across the EEA (GDPR's objective).
- Ensure consistent application of the GDPR across member states (the EDPB's coordination mandate).
- Advise on data-protection matters and promote public awareness (GDPR Art 57 duties of supervisory authorities).
- Handle complaints, conduct investigations, and impose corrective powers including administrative fines (GDPR Art 58).

## What they do

- Supervise controllers and processors established or offering services in the EU/EEA (GDPR Art 3 territorial scope).
- Issue guidelines, recommendations, and best-practice opinions (EDPB; binding on authorities under GDPR Art 64/65 procedures).
- Operate the one-stop-shop cooperation and consistency mechanisms for cross-border processing.
- Investigate breaches, order remedies, and impose fines up to the GDPR's administrative-fine tiers.

## Engagement with the enterprise

The enterprise's public repositories and community site process personal data in ways that can engage GDPR obligations; the enforced-regime record (`enforces` -> the GDPR sibling record) anchors the relationship. The enterprise's privacy capabilities and services are operated to remain answerable to this collective.
