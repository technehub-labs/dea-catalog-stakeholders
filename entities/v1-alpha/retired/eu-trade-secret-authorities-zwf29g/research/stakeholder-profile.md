## Definition

The EU member-state trade-secret competent authorities are the national courts and authorities that enforce Directive (EU) 2016/943 (the Trade Secrets Directive): the regime protecting undisclosed know-how and business information against unlawful acquisition, use, and disclosure. Enforcement is judicial: member-state courts grant injunctions, damages, and interim measures; the CJEU provides binding interpretation.

The record's normative description (verbatim): *The EU member-state competent authorities and courts that enforce Directive (EU) 2016/943 on the protection of trade secrets as transposed into national law.*

## Mandate and goals

- Protect lawful controllers of trade secrets against unlawful acquisition, use, and disclosure (the directive's objective).
- Provide effective judicial remedies (injunctions, damages, corrective measures) in every member state.
- Balance protection against fundamental rights and public interest (whistleblower, journalistic, and worker protections in the directive).

## What they do

- Adjudicate trade-secret misappropriation actions in member-state courts (civil remedies under the directive's Arts 12-15).
- Grant interim and precautionary measures (including injunctions and, where appropriate, seizure of goods).
- Refer interpretation questions to the CJEU (preliminary rulings under Art 267 TFEU).

## Engagement with the enterprise

The enterprise's undisclosed know-how (architecture models, research) is protected in the EU under the regime this class enforces; the enforced-regime record anchors the relationship. In-repo, the Apache-2.0 license deliberately publishes parts of the corpus while the regime protects what remains undisclosed.
