## Definition

The California Privacy Protection Agency is the state's dedicated consumer-privacy regulator — the first of its kind in the United States — with rulemaking, enforcement, and administrative-fine authority under the CCPA as amended by the CPRA. The California Attorney General shares enforcement of the CCPA (and enforced it exclusively before the CPPA became operational). Together they are the enforcing set for California consumer-privacy law.

The record's normative description (verbatim): *The California Privacy Protection Agency and the California Attorney General, the two enforcing authorities for the CCPA as amended by the CPRA, with rulemaking and enforcement powers over the enterprise's California-facing processing.*

## Mandate and goals

- Protect California consumers' privacy rights (the CCPA/CPRA's objective: knowing, deleting, correcting, and opting out of the sale/sharing of personal information).
- Promote public awareness of privacy rights and industry compliance.
- Adopt, update, and enforce regulations implementing the CCPA/CPRA (the CPPA's rulemaking mandate).

## What they do

- CPPA: rulemaking on CCPA/CPRA implementation (notices, opt-outs, risk assessments, audits, cybersecurity programs).
- CPPA + AG: investigations and enforcement actions, including administrative fines (CPPA) and civil penalties (AG).
- AG: publishes CCPA enforcement guidance and operates the enforcement program it ran from 2020.
- Both: outreach and technical assistance to businesses subject to the Act.

## Engagement with the enterprise

The enterprise's public processing surfaces can engage CCPA/CPRA obligations where California consumers' personal information is involved; the enforced-regime record anchors the relationship, and the enterprise's privacy capabilities are operated to remain answerable to this authority.
